When someone who uses a wheelchair full-time moves into a new place, grab bars aren't decoration. They are the anchor points for every **transfer**: chair to toilet, chair to tub bench, chair to bed. This post covers where to put them, what heights to use, how to mount them in condo walls, and specific products from Home Depot and Lowe's.

The dimensions below follow the **2010 ADA Standards** (sections 604 and 607). Residential work doesn't have to meet ADA, but those numbers are well tested and a good starting point. Adjust them to the person. Watch them do a transfer and mount the bar where their hand naturally goes.

## First: How Does the Person Transfer?

Ask this before you buy anything. It decides what you install.

| Transfer style | What matters most |
|---|---|
| **Stand-pivot** (can bear weight briefly) | Vertical bars and transfer poles to pull up on |
| **Sliding / transfer board** (no standing) | Horizontal bars at seat level, fold-down bars, a tub transfer bench |
| **Dependent** (caregiver or lift) | Clear floor space and a lift. Bars are secondary |

## Bathroom (Do This First)

### Toilet

- **Side wall:** a 42" horizontal bar at 33–36" above the floor. Start it no more than 12" from the back wall, and run it at least 54" out from the back wall.
- **Rear wall:** a 36" horizontal bar at 33–36". Extend it at least 12" past the toilet centerline on the wall side and 24" on the transfer side.
- **Open side (no wall):** a **swing-up or fold-down bar**. Wheelchair users often need to transfer from the side, so it has to fold out of the way.

### Tub

- **Back wall:** two horizontal bars. One goes at 33–36" above the floor and one 8–10" above the tub rim.
- **Faucet (control) end:** a 24" horizontal bar at the front edge of the tub.
- **Entry end:** an 18–24" **vertical** bar on the wall at the open end, for steadying during the transfer.
- Pair the bars with a **sliding tub transfer bench** and a **handheld shower on a slide bar**. For a wheelchair user, the bench is what makes the tub usable. The bars help.

## Bedroom

- A **floor-to-ceiling tension pole** (SuperPole style) next to the bed on the transfer side. It needs no drilling, which is good in a condo. Check that the ceiling height falls within the pole's range, usually 93–99".
- Or a **bed-mounted assist rail** that slides under the mattress.
- A trapeze attachment helps someone reposition in bed using their upper body.

## Elsewhere

- **Entry door:** a vertical bar on the latch side for leverage while opening the door.
- **Lever handles** on every door instead of knobs. It's cheap and makes a big difference.
- **Closet or kitchen:** a horizontal bar wherever she'll lean from the chair to reach.

## Mounting: The Part That Actually Matters

A grab bar is only as strong as what it's screwed into.

| Wall type | Method |
|---|---|
| Wood studs | Screw directly into studs (3 screws per flange). Moen SecureMount flanges let you angle screws to catch an off-center stud |
| Hollow drywall, no stud | **WingIts** structural anchors or Moen SecureMount anchors (sold separately) |
| Steel studs (common in condos) | Open the wall and add 2x wood blocking, or use WingIts rated for hollow walls |
| Concrete / block | Sleeve or wedge anchors, per the bar maker's instructions |
| Fiberglass tub surround | WingIts anchors rated for surrounds. Never rely on the surround alone |

Rules of thumb:

- Bars should be **1¼"–1½" in diameter** with **1½" of clearance** from the wall.
- Pick bars **rated 500 lb**. Use anchors rated at least as high as the bar.
- **Concealed-screw flanges** look finished. Exposed-screw bars are cheaper and just as strong.
- If you open walls, block the **entire** bathroom at 30–40" high while it's open. That way future bars can go anywhere.
- **Condo:** check the HOA rules before cutting into walls, especially walls shared with a neighbor or walls with plumbing.

## Paying for It

Before buying everything out of pocket, check for help with home modifications:

- **Area Agency on Aging.** In Ohio, the regional agency can screen for programs that cover modifications.
- **Medicaid waiver programs** (Ohio's PASSPORT program for adults 60+). Approved home modifications can be covered.
- **Occupational therapist home assessment.** Often covered by Medicare or insurance with a doctor's order. An OT will mark exact bar positions for the person's body and transfer style, which is worth more than any chart.

## Install Checklist

1. Watch the person transfer and mark hand positions with painter's tape.
2. Find the studs and identify the wall type (stud finder, magnet, test hole behind where the bar flange will go).
3. Pick the anchor method for each wall.
4. Level and mark the bar, drill, and mount.
5. **Load-test every bar** with your full body weight, pulling down and out, before she uses it.
6. Re-check for loosening after the first month.

A tractor that has sat for a decade can usually be brought back, but the order of operations matters. Rush to the key and you risk a hydrolocked cylinder, a scored crank bearing, or a carburetor full of varnish. The sequence is: **fluids and fuel first, turn it by hand, crank without firing, then start.**

## 1. Inspect Before Touching the Key

- Check for rodent nests in the air cleaner, intake, exhaust, and wiring harness. Chewed wiring is common after long storage.
- If the exhaust stack is uncapped, check it for water or rust. Water down the stack can rust a valve or cylinder.
- Check the tires for dry rot and sidewall cracks, then inflate or replace them.

## 2. Drain and Replace the Fuel

- Drain the tank completely. Ten-year-old gasoline turns to varnish, and old diesel grows algae and collects water.
- Pull and clean the sediment bowl and screen.
- Replace the fuel filters and any cracked or soft fuel lines.
- **Gas engines:** plan on cleaning or rebuilding the carburetor.
- **Diesel engines:** bleed the air from the fuel system per the operator's manual.

## 3. Oil, Coolant, and Hydraulics

- Drain the engine oil and look for milky oil (a coolant leak) or free water. Refill it and replace the filter.
- Check the coolant level and condition. Inspect the hoses and water pump seal, which crack and dry out.
- Check the transmission and hydraulic fluid. Water settles to the bottom, so crack the drain plug and let a little out to check.

## 4. Free the Engine by Hand

- Remove the spark plugs (gas) or the injectors or glow plugs (diesel).
- Put about a teaspoon of engine oil or Marvel Mystery Oil in each cylinder and let it soak overnight.
- Turn the crank by hand with a breaker bar.

> **If it won't turn, do not force it and do not use the starter.** Keep soaking and gently rocking it back and forth. A stuck engine freed patiently is often fine; one forced with the starter may not be.

## 5. Build Oil Pressure Before Firing

- Install a fresh, fully charged battery, and clean the cables and terminals.
- With the plugs or injectors still out, crank in 10–15 second bursts until the oil pressure gauge moves. This lubricates the bearings before the engine sees any load.

## 6. First Start

- Install new spark plugs (gas) or reinstall the injectors (diesel). Check the points and condenser if the engine has them.
- Start it and idle low. **Confirm oil pressure within seconds, and shut down immediately if there is none.**
- Watch for leaks, temperature, and smoke. Some blue smoke early on is normal as the rings free up.

## 7. After It Runs

- Run it briefly, then change the oil again. The first run picks up sludge.
- Test the brakes, clutch, and PTO before putting any load on the tractor.

## Quick Reference

| Phase | Key Action | Don't Skip |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| Inspect | Rodent nests, exhaust, tires | Wiring damage |
| Fuel | Drain tank, filters, lines | Carb rebuild / diesel bleed |
| Fluids | Oil, coolant, hydraulics | Check for water |
| Free Engine | Oil in cylinders, turn by hand | Never force a stuck engine |
| Oil Pressure | Crank with plugs out | Gauge must move first |
| First Start | Idle low, watch gauges | Shut down on no pressure |
| After Running | Second oil change, test controls | Brakes before load |

## Notes

Procedures vary by make, model, and fuel type, especially fuel system bleeding and carburetor service. Keep the operator's or service manual for your specific tractor on hand.

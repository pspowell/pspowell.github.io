Sweet, spicy, tangy candied jalapeño slices — great on burgers, crackers with cream cheese, or straight from the jar. Makes about 3-4 pints.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Jalapeños, sliced into 1/4-inch rounds | 2 lb |
| Apple cider vinegar | 2 cups |
| Granulated sugar | 4 cups |
| Garlic powder | 1 tsp |
| Celery seed | 1 tsp |
| Turmeric | 1/2 tsp |
| Cayenne pepper | 1/4 tsp |

## Instructions

1. **Slice jalapeños** — Wash and slice the jalapeños into even 1/4-inch rounds. Wear gloves if you're sensitive to the oils. Set aside.
2. **Make the syrup** — In a large pot, combine the vinegar, sugar, garlic powder, celery seed, turmeric, and cayenne. Bring to a boil, stirring until the sugar dissolves. (~5 min)
3. **Simmer syrup** — Reduce heat and let the syrup simmer gently for 5 minutes to let the flavors meld.
4. **Add jalapeños** — Add the sliced jalapeños to the syrup and simmer for 4 minutes — just enough to soften slightly and infuse flavor.
5. **Pack jars** — Using a slotted spoon, pack the jalapeño slices into clean pint jars, leaving 1/4-inch headspace.
6. **Boil syrup down** — Return the remaining syrup to a boil and let it reduce for another 5 minutes until slightly thickened.
7. **Fill jars with syrup** — Pour the hot syrup over the jalapeños in the jars, leaving 1/4-inch headspace. Remove air bubbles and wipe rims clean.
8. **Process (if canning)** — For shelf-stable jars, process in a boiling water bath for 10 minutes. Otherwise, seal and refrigerate.
9. **Cool and cure** — Let jars cool completely, then refrigerate. Flavor improves after resting at least 24 hours — 1 week is even better.

## Notes

If not processing in a water bath, store in the fridge — they'll keep for a couple months. For shelf-stable jars, make sure jars and lids are properly sterilized and follow standard water-bath canning safety practices. Great on nachos, burgers, cornbread, or with cream cheese and crackers.
